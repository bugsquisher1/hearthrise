HEARTHRISE — blind panel, player: "the Steam skeptic"
Played 2026-10-31. Desktop browser, 1920x1080, mouse and keyboard.
Build: origin/main c374ad2 + lane/t-local-world + claude/upbeat-einstein-mrh0ig + claude/serene-tesla-7txs5f
(all three merged clean, no conflicts), played through tools/qa/local-world.mjs.

Who I am: I've refunded a lot of games. Asset-flip idlers, AI-looking dashboards, "early access"
that never finished. I give a game 30 minutes, then I write the review. I'm fair. I'm not patient.

Minute marks are my play clock: real time on screen plus any time I spent "away" (world.skip).
Shot names refer to the screenshots in skeptic/ (only the best 15 are committed, downscaled).
Fallback fonts are a sandbox artifact (no Google Fonts here); I don't count them against the game.

[0:00] FIRST SCREEN — sign-in. (01-sign-in, 02-crest-zoom)
  Doing: opened the game.
  Expected: a title screen that tells me what kind of game this is. Some art, a peek at the world,
  maybe a tagline.
  Got: a gold crest on the left, a small sign-in box on the right, and a lot of black. About 70% of
  a 1920x1080 screen is empty. No world, no character, no tagline, no sound, nothing moving.
  Felt: wary right away. I zoomed into the crest. The sun has tree-ring texture, the scrollwork is
  soft, mirrored filler, and the shading is smooth airbrushed brass. It reads like an AI-generated
  logo, which is the exact thing I've been burned by. It's also not pixel art, so I have no idea
  yet what the game itself will look like. Page loaded in about 1.4 s, so no complaint there.
  The sign-in box is tasteful: a thin steel frame, square pixel corner studs, a faint dot texture.
  That frame is the first thing that looks like someone chose it.

[0:30] CREATE ACCOUNT. (03-create-account, 04-create-weak-password, 05-account-created)
  Doing: tried "hunter2" like I always do.
  Got: "Use at least 10 characters." in red. Clear and fast. The hint line above says the same thing
  ("At least 10 characters."), so the message is printed twice, but fine.
  Then: "Check your email for a link to finish creating your account." An email-confirm wall before
  I've seen one pixel of the game. On Steam this would be a Steam login, so I'll let it go for the
  browser build, but in a browser it's a real drop-off point. (The local world confirms at once.)
  Small thing: "If you already have an account, sign in instead." is plain text, not a link. The
  link is at the bottom.

[0:50] CHARACTER SELECT. (06-after-sign-in)
  Got: "Your characters. Signed in as skeptic@local-world.test. Up to 4 can play at once." and then

  Felt: still a form in a black void. Three screens in, and I haven't seen the game yet.

[1:10] CREATE YOUR HERO. (07-create-hero, 10-hero-dressed)
  Doing: making a character.
  Got: a name box, four rows of 19 colour swatches (head/body/legs/feet), a little paper-doll, and a

  The good: "There's no class to pick. The weapon you hold is how you fight." That's one clear

  I respect that a lot more than a 4-page class picker.
  The less good: it's still a wall of reading before I've played a second. And the paper-doll is a
  very crude, static 16-px mannequin with black lines down its torso for arms. No idle bob, no turn.
  Colours change instantly, which is nice.
  The layout jumps: the crest slides left to make room. Not a big deal, it just doesn't feel planned.

[1:50] ENTER TOWN — first look at the actual game. (12-town-arrival)

  Then, within ten seconds:
  - MY HERO ISN'T MY HERO. (13-town-hero-zoom) I made a grey-haired guy in a teal shirt and dark trousers.
    The world shows a full-plate knight with a red plume and a blue-and-gold shield. None of my
    colours are on him. Then why did I spend a minute picking them? The paper-doll and the world
    sprite are also two different art styles (crude chibi vs. a detailed sprite).
  - Default zoom is way in. The first screen is mostly three different stone textures side by side
    at different scales: small light-grey bricks, a dark strip, giant flagstones. (14-town-ground-zoom)
    The light-grey brick road is flat and low-contrast and looks like a placeholder tile. The knight's

  - The HUD bar: "Offens...", "Balanc...", "Defen..." truncated, at 1920 px wide. There is room.

  - The opening quest card is short and plain: "Rats in the mill. The miller has rats in his grain.

[7:50] THE ACTION BAR TIP, and the thing that might actually be this game's hook.
  (33-slot-6-picker, 34-slot-6-condition, 36-bar-after-save)
  Tip: "Your hero uses this bar by itself. It tries the slots in order and uses the first one
  that's ready and whose conditions are all true." Clicking an empty slot opens "Configure action":

  Polish: the spell table uses 60% of the dialog's width, yet the Rune column wraps "1 / Rupture /
  rune" onto three lines. Spell icons don't match: most are framed squares, Mend and Rend are tiny
  unframed glyphs that look unfinished. The detail pane showed "Power Strike. Costs only mana.
  Requires: Shield." while Mend was the one selected.

  1. The Great Hearth loop. Rats drop bones, bones fuel the hearth in the middle of town, the hearth

  3. The action bar condition editor ("Cast Mend when my HP is below 50%", "allies under", "my target

HANDCRAFTED OR GENERATED?
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Outside the game (sign-in, characters, creator): it looks generated. A black void, an airbrushed
  AI-style crest, a crude paper-doll. Inside the world: handcrafted, and consistent within itself
  (one top-down perspective, one muted palette), though the town floor mixes three stone textures
  at different scales, the water is straight-edged rectangles, the knight's pixels are finer than
  the road's, and smooth round light blobs sit on top of the pixel art. The windows are a dense,
  competent MMO client, not a generated dashboard, except the Shop, which is a list.
  Would I recognise it from a screenshot without the logo? The hearth plaza and the gatehouse, yes.

  Placeholder-looking: "To the sandbox" signpost; the two-grey-box farm; turnips fully grown at

  👍 RECOMMENDED (barely). 0.5 hrs on record.

  Hearthrise hides a real game under a spreadsheet. The first three screens are a black void and a
  crest that looks like it came out of an image generator. The character creator lets you pick
  four colours that appear nowhere. At minute nine the tutorial has you doing arithmetic on shrimp.
  I had the refund page open.

  Then it clicks. Every hunting ground is a place someone built: a cellar full of grain sacks under a
  mill with a rat problem, a wolf thicket with an axe left in a stump. Monsters drop bones, you burn
  them in the Great Hearth in the town square, and that makes you tougher. The name means something.
  You can program your hero's action bar with real conditions. It's fast, it never crashed, and
  your hero really does keep hunting after you close the tab.

  But coming back is silent, half the windows are walls of numbers, and labels are cut off at
  1080p. Buy it for the hearth, not the UI.

  Refund minute: none. Closest call: 9:40 to 10:20, the shrimp-threshold tutorial.
