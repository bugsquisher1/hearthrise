How this projection works

We look at the world from the front, slightly above. Anything level stays level: a wall's top and bottom edges, both shoulders, both eyes, both feet sit on one pixel row, as if the front face is seen straight on. Only the up direction is tilted: every vertical edge leans 45° to the left, so the top of anything sits up-left of its bottom, exactly 1 px left for every 1 px up. That lean is what shows the side and the top of objects without real perspective. Ground tiles are flat squares. Light comes from the upper left, so shadows fall down-right.

Tips for drawing in this style

  • Draw the front face flat first, as a plain elevation. Then lean only the verticals.
  • Use clean one-pixel stair steps on every leaning edge (1 left, 1 up). No 2:1 or wobbly steps.
  • Keep the feet or base row fixed; the lean grows with height, so tall things (houses, lampposts) visibly slant and short things barely do.
  • Keep outlines thin and selective: dark only where a form meets shadow or the ground. That gives the gritty classic look instead of thick cartoon outlines.
  • Light the top-left planes, darken the right and bottom; cast shadows down-right on the ground.
  • Scale check: a hero is about 1.2 tiles (≈38 px at T=32); a house is 3-4+ tiles tall.
