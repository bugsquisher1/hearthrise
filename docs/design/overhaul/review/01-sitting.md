# The sitting: ten questions for Tyler

Written 2026-11-21 on `lane/d-critical-review-main`. This is step 2 of the critical review (`00-critical-review.md`, section 8). Docs only.

Each question is a place where two of your answers disagree, or where a design was written after you answered and you have not seen it. No option is recommended. Each option says what it costs the player, the economy and the build.

Labels. MEASURED is a run on the real engine or a count, and says how thin it was. COMPUTED is arithmetic on ruled numbers. JUDGMENT is an opinion. UNKNOWN means nobody knows. The same text is in `01-sitting.json` for the Rulings page.

## S-1. On-call and hired heroes

You kept on-call heroes on the cut list, and you let "no hired, lent or on-call heroes" stand on the reversal list. Which one is the game?

- **A. Keep them.** Your 2026-11-20 ruling stands: a hero can be on call for about 2 hours a day, 4 at most, with an on-call board. Players get one way to help each other while asleep. The build gains a new server-owned surface where one account's hero earns inside another account's hunt, which the Security review must cover. COMPUTED: among four real players only one pair in six was close enough in Level to share XP. UNKNOWN whether anyone uses it, because every account now has four heroes of its own.
- **B. Remove them.** Nothing is built. The only way two players hunt together is an invite by hand while both are there. JUDGMENT: this removes one of the few ways a player can need another player. It reverses your 2026-11-20 ruling.
- **C. Hide for now.** Not built in the first build and not cut. It comes back if playtest friends ask to lend heroes without being prompted. It costs nothing now. The risk is that nobody asks for a thing they have never seen. JUDGMENT.

## S-2. The party finder and Local chat

You kept the live party finder and the Local chat tab on the cut list, and you let their removal stand on the reversal list. Which one is the game?

- **A. Keep both.** Your rulings of 2026-10-15 and 2026-11-11 stand: a finder filtered by weapon, and town criers calling tips in Local chat. Both need two people at the screen in the same minute. MEASURED, thin: four testers over 33 days pressed in the same ten minutes in 5 of 675 windows. So on a small server both may sit empty, which can make the game look dead.
- **B. Remove both.** Global, Clan, Party and whispers stay. Town criers speak in Global and in the town itself. Players find each other through clans and friends only. Less to build. JUDGMENT: a new player has no tool for finding a group of strangers.
- **C. Local only.** Keep the Local tab for town criers and people in town. Drop the live finder. Small build. Criers keep their own home as you ruled on 2026-11-11. The finder question comes back only if players ask. JUDGMENT.

## S-3. Priced setup slots

You kept priced setup slots on the cut list, and you let "setup slots are free" stand on the reversal list. Which one is the game?

- **A. Keep priced.** Your 2026-11-05 ruling stands: three free, then 15,000 and 40,000 gold. COMPUTED: 55,000 gold a hero, 220,000 for four heroes, once. The book now wants one saved Setup for each ground, so a hero that hunts more than three grounds meets the price. A player then pays to stop repeating a decision by hand.
- **B. Free.** Every saved Setup is free. The gold sink of about 220,000 an account goes. It fits the first principle that a choice made once is remembered. It reverses your 2026-11-05 ruling and one line of your 2026-11-04 sinks list.
- **C. Free per ground.** One Setup for each ground is free. Extra Setups for the same ground are priced as ruled. The sink stays for players who want several builds, and nobody pays to remember a ground. A little more to build than either other option. JUDGMENT.

## S-4. The clan library and a Level XP room

You kept the clan library and Level XP room on the cut list. Your 2026-11-20 ruling says clans give no Level XP. Which one is the game?

- **A. XP library.** A clan room pays a standing Level XP bonus. It reverses your 2026-11-20 ruling. COMPUTED: at +5% the top pace reading moves from 1.65 to about 1.73 times a plain four, and every clan member levels faster than the Level table all the time. The next region must then open sooner. Being in no clan becomes a real cost.
- **B. Library, no XP.** The library stays as the castle's fifth building and pays something that is not Level XP. Your ruling holds. Somebody has to design what it pays, and that design is new and unplayed. UNKNOWN what it would be.
- **C. No library.** The castle has four buildings: damage, protection, town work speed and rations. Your ruling holds and nothing new is designed. The castle is shorter by one building.

## S-5. What a death costs at high Level

In the morning you picked a capped death. In the afternoon you let the Blessing ladder stand. Earlier you ruled 6% flat with no cap. Which one is the game?

- **A. 6%, no cap.** Three Blessings and nothing more. COMPUTED from the Level table: a fully blessed death costs about 5.5 hunted hours at Level 100, 13 at Level 200 and 38 at Level 400. Unblessed it is 22, 53 and 150. The fear stays real for ever. COMPUTED on an assumed death count: a blessed four on Deadly then earns less than the same heroes on Normal from about Level 100 to 145 on.
- **B. Blessing ladder.** 6% flat stays. Past the third Blessing a new one opens whenever a blessed death would cost over five hunted hours. COMPUTED: a blessed death then costs about 3 to 5 hours at every Level, and an unblessed death keeps its full cost. It is paid in gold, so it is a sink that grows with Level. For a player who pays it is softer than the 12 hour cap you turned down. The five hours is a guess until a real death rate exists.
- **C. Capped death.** A death never costs more than a set number of hunted hours. The version you were shown before was 48 hours unblessed and 12 with three Blessings. COMPUTED: that cap starts to bite at about Level 190. Below that nothing changes. No new Blessings to build and no new gold sink. An unblessed death stops growing, which the other two options keep.

## S-6. Is Deadly for idle players

You ruled that a mid-geared four on Deadly dies 50 to 100 times in 1,000 hours. A death ends the hunt and no hunt starts by itself. So a death while you are away also costs the rest of that stamina bar. Is that what you want?

- **A. Yes, accept it.** Deadly is a tier you watch, or gamble on. COMPUTED from your death band, with all four coming home on a death: on the free 8 hour bar 33 to 55% of away sessions end early and 18 to 31% of hunting time is lost. On Premium's 12 hour bar 45 to 70% end early and 25 to 42% is lost. That is before the 6% of Level XP. An idle player will mostly use Dangerous. Deadly can then not be the best way for an idle four to level, and Premium's long bar is worth least on the hardest tier.
- **B. Fewer deaths.** Lower the Deadly death band so an away session usually finishes. COMPUTED: for 9 in 10 free sessions to finish, the band must be about 13 deaths in 1,000 hours, which is where Dangerous is ruled today. It changes your 2026-11-21 ruling. Deadly keeps its bigger pulls and harder blows and becomes less of a gamble. JUDGMENT: it may stop feeling like a struggle.
- **C. Others hunt on.** Keep the death band. When one hero dies the other three keep hunting by default, which is your 2026-11-20 ruling for an account's own heroes. Less time is lost. The three hunt without their tank or their healer, so one death may lead to more. UNKNOWN how often, because the simulator's dead hero stands up at once and cannot show it.

## S-7. The clan castle

You kept the castle's old parts (labor, votes, upkeep, treasury, Clan Standing, five buildings of ten levels), and you let the simple castle priced by member stand. Both were then written into one design you have not seen. Which castle is the game?

- **A. Simple castle.** One project at a time, four buildings of five levels, priced for each member with a floor of five. No labor, no upkeep, no votes. COMPUTED: 20 to 26 weeks to finish. Small build on the town project engine. A clan that finishes it has only looks and the weekly goal left. It reverses your 2026-11-20 castle rulings, and it is the option you declined once.
- **B. Full castle.** Five buildings of ten levels, with labor paid in rested town hours, a weekly upkeep that switches the buffs off when unpaid, a vote for the next project and a treasury. COMPUTED: about a year to finish. A long shared goal and a larger gold sink. Much more to build and to secure, and castle labor competes with training and gathering for the same 12 rested hours. All its sizes are a HYPOTHESIS written the same day.
- **C. Decide later.** Clans ship with a roster, chat and a browser. The castle stays hidden and unbuilt, as you ruled on 2026-11-20, until clans exist and hold members. Nothing is cut. Clans pay no buffs at first, which is less than your 2026-11-21 direction that clans pay buffs. No gold sink from the castle in the first months.

## S-8. Is there a ceiling on what paying buys

You said yes to "paying gives an edge under a ceiling written in numbers, and nobody passes it". You then said no to the gear ceiling: "paying can put a hero way ahead, it depends on what is on the player market". Both are printed as ruled. Which one does the game promise?

- **A. No ceiling.** The game promises no ceiling on gear or gold bought through other players. The limits that remain are structural: gear is worn by Level, the store sells no gear, and an account may sell only so many Crowns a week. The book stops saying "nobody passes it". A payer's lead is whatever the market holds. UNKNOWN how big that is, because no market has run.
- **B. Pace ceiling only.** The game promises a ceiling on buffs and Level pace and none on gear. COMPUTED: 1.65 times a plain four with every cap full, about 1.22 from paying alone. No tool has ever played a buff, so those numbers are a product of targets. The promise is narrower and can be held by the buff caps you already approved.
- **C. Written gear ceiling.** The game promises that a payer is never more than best gear over mid gear inside a band. To keep that promise the game needs a rule that limits what bought gold can buy, which you ruled against on 2026-11-21 (no limit on how much sooner). More to build and to police. It is the easiest promise to explain to a player who does not pay. JUDGMENT.

## S-9. The rested bank

You kept the rested bank on the cut list. It was described to you as moving no number. It was then re-designed to pay +50% Level XP on up to 24 missed hunted hours. You have not seen that design. What is the rested bank?

- **A. Pays +50%.** A hero that misses hunted hours gets +50% Level XP on that many later hours, up to 24. COMPUTED: a free player who visits once a day hunts 8 hours, misses 4 and gets 2 hours' worth back, which is half of what Premium's 12 hour bar sells. A missed day returns 6 hours' worth. It softens a missed day. It is a new Level XP bonus that is not on your reversal list and has not been played. UNKNOWN whether it teaches players to skip days.
- **B. Keep it inert.** The code stays, hidden, and pays nothing. MEASURED: switching it off moved no number in 207 simulated cells. Nothing is cut and nothing new is designed. It can be designed later with real numbers on how often players miss days.
- **C. Cut it.** The rested bank is removed, which was the first recommendation. The Level table is the only clock, and a missed day is simply missed. Least to build and to explain. It reverses your "keep it".

## S-10. Party XP and different classes

Your 2026-10-31 ruling says party XP is better than solo only when the party holds different classes. The option you picked for Deadly ended with "pay party XP by size alone". That ends the classes rule, and nobody said so on the page. Do you mean to end it?

- **A. Size alone.** A four earns the same party bonus whatever it holds. One simple table, as you ruled on 2026-11-16. MEASURED, one seed and one hour, direction only: a hammer and three mages was the fastest four at Level 60. So the best party may be one damage type, and nothing in the XP pays a player for fielding a tank and a healer. The pre-filled starting company and the "one job for each seat" design lose their reason.
- **B. Keep classes.** The party table still pays more for a mix of classes, as one global table. Your 2026-10-31 ruling stands. A mixed four is always the better choice by XP, so the choice is made for the player. Four of the same weapon earn less XP together than apart. JUDGMENT: it is a rule a player has to be told, where size alone needs no telling.
- **C. Size now, look later.** Size alone for the first build. After Deadly is sized on a fixed measuring tool, read which fours players really run. If one damage type wins everywhere, the classes rule can return as one row of the table. Costs nothing extra now. The first players may all learn to run four mages. JUDGMENT.
