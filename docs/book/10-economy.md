# 10. The economy

Gold, the wage, what you buy and sell, Hunting blocks, every gold sink, the Weighhouse and the market to come.

---

## 10.1 Gold
**[Unchanged]**

- Gold lives in one **account purse**, shared by every hero.
- You start with 500.
- The server owns every coin. Every gain and spend is written to a ledger.
- Crowns are a separate currency bought with real money. They're outside 0.3 (chapter 14).

## 10.2 The wage
**[New in 0.3]**

The wage is what one normal hunted hour pays a hero at a Level, in coin and loot sold to the store.

| Level | One hunted hour pays about |
|---|---|
| 60 | 16,365 gold |
| 80 | 17,726 gold |
| 100 | 19,199 gold |
| 120 | 20,795 gold |
| 200 | 28,619 gold |

- It grows about 0.4% a Level. Level XP grows faster than gold, on purpose, so a far player can't buy a whole early market.
- **Prices are set in hours of wage.** A per-hero cost (a Hunting block, a Talent reset) reads your hero's Level. Goods and gear read their own tier or band.
- So a price feels the same at Level 20 and Level 2,000.

**By Risk setting,** the gold estimate per hour is about 0.63 of Steady on Careful and 1.27 of Steady on Bold.

Not final: every price in hours of wage is a starting value. A hunted hour's supplies take about a quarter of its pay, so it nets about 0.75 hours of wage.

## 10.5 Hunting blocks
**[New in 0.3]**

- Two more hours of hunting, bought with gold.
- **At most 2 a day** per hero. So the most anyone hunts is 14 hours a day, 16 with Premium.
- Each block costs 1.5 times the one before it that server day. The count resets at the server's midnight.
- **Pretty pricey on purpose.** A block hour costs about 2 hours of wage, about twice what it earns back. You buy XP, drops and remains with it, never more gold.
- A Level 60 hero's first block costs about **65,460 gold**, the second about **98,190**. A Steady hour at Level 60 pays about 16,300.
- Bought time can sit above your normal day, but never past one day's worth of blocks.
- Offered on the return card. A standing "buy up to N" rule in the plan buys them for you.

These replace the **stamina refill** (4 hours, up to 6 a day, 1,000 gold + 250 a Level, half again for each one that day), which goes away in 0.3.

## 10.9 The market
**[Later patch]**

There's **no market in 0.3**. Trait pieces are kept, carried over or sold to the store. Remains burn or sell to the store. The design ships on its own later.

**How it will play:** your plan buys what your heroes burn and sells what they drop, all day, through one book at the Weighhouse, while you're away. When you open the window you see what everything is worth, what sold, and who just sold a Very Rare for a fortune.

- **One book for the realm.** Buy and sell orders, matched by the server the moment they cross. Nobody needs to be online.
- **The plan is the trader.** Stock rules become standing buy orders. Loot rules become standing sell orders.
- **Stacks trade on the book. Trait pieces trade one by one,** with "wanted" orders: "any Rare ring with this Trait at rank II or better, up to this price" fills the moment one is listed.
- **A price guide** everyone trusts: the median of real sales, with a history line. The hunt card shows what an hour's drops sell for.
- **Fees are small and burned:** 4% on a sale, 1% on what rests on the book.
- **Limits** on how much one player can buy in 4 hours, price bands around each item's catalogue value, and a hold on very big payouts.
- **Big sales are told in town.** Every Signature sale is.
- **Flippers** are a real playstyle with their own record.
- Every tradeable already carries its catalogue value in 0.3, so the market lands with no rebalance.

Not final: a storefront version of the market is staged in the database today. The design above replaces it.

## 10.10 Trading with players
**[Later patch]**

- No direct trade between players in 0.3. The market is the way.
- Every piece of gear will trade. Store-bought items other than Crown items can't be traded.
- Hearthfinds and other bound items never trade.
