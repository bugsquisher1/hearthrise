### S-1 — the Vigour charge is a function of how often you settle **[P0, BLOCK]**

Design §5 states the property this whole feature rests on, in italics of its own:

> "Vigour is charged from the same `ms` the payout is computed from, in the same transaction. A
> window cannot pay and not charge, **because there is one number**."

`accrual.js` already states the payout half of that as a conservation law, one line above the
`ACCRUE_MIN_MS` floor it was written to justify:

> "WHY THIS CANNOT BE GAMED. Paying it grants exactly what the simulation computes for the elapsed
> span and advances `accrued_to` to `now()`, so **time is conserved: switching twice pays the same
> total as switching once**."  — `accrual.js:1467-1472`

The charge does not obey that law. `src/core/hunt.js:375`:

```js
export function vigourChargeMin(windowMs) {
  const ms = Math.floor(Number(windowMs) || 0);
  return ms > 0 ? Math.floor(ms / 60000) : 0;
}
```

`floor`, **per window**, and the remainder is discarded. There is no carry column, no remainder on
the ledger row, and nothing anywhere that re-reads the lost milliseconds — I searched the repo for
one. `accrual.js:2926` proposes the floored value and nothing else:

```js
const charge = vigourChargeMin(grantMs);
if (charge > 0) progress.push({ kind:'daily', key: VIGOUR_PROGRESS_KEY, period: …, add: charge, … });
```

The file's own comment calls this "the same direction every other rounding in this engine takes —
toward the player". Every other rounding is a **payout** rounding. This one is a **limiter**, and
rounding a limiter toward the player is not generosity, it is the limiter not existing.

**Measured, against the real `computeAccrual`, on this branch** (`tests/vigour-charge-conservation.mjs`,
five arms, exit 1):

| how one hour of combat is settled | caller | paid | **Vigour charged** | gold |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| one 3,600,000 ms window | `accrue` | 60.0 min | **60** | 4,174 |
| 40 × 90,000 ms | `accrue` — **no privilege, any client may poll at 90 s** | 60.0 min | **40** | 3,840 |
| 61 × 59,000 ms | `collect` — what **every `set_activity`** passes | 60.0 min | **0** | **4,209** |
| 40 × 90,000 ms | `tick` — the shipped 90 s flush | 60.0 min | **40** | — |
| 80 × 45,000 ms | `tick` — `flush_seconds` tuned to 45 | 60.0 min | **0** | — |

Three separate ways in, in rising order of how much an attacker has to know:

1. **Nobody does anything wrong.** A 90 s poll cadence is above `ACCRUE_MIN_MS` and is an ordinary
   `accrue`. It is a flat **33% discount on the daily limiter**, systematically, for every player
   whose client happens to poll there.
2. **Switch-spam, and it is unbounded.** `runSetActivity` passes `caller:'collect'`
   (`set-activity.js:1111`), which is exempt from `ACCRUE_MIN_MS` by design (b531) and pays any
   sub-minute window that produced value (`set-activity.js:1154` only discards windows with *no*
   value). A client looping `set_activity` at 59 s **hunts at full rate forever**: the meter never
   moves, `spent_min` stays 0, `remaining_min` stays at the grant, the 22-hour ceiling is never
   approached and the refill ladder sells something nobody needs. Note the third row also pays
   **more gold** than the single window — 4,209 vs 4,174 — so this is not even a trade.
3. **An operator knob decides whether the limiter exists.** `caller:'tick'` is exempt from the floor
   too (`tick-shadow.js:149`), and `hr_tick_config.flush_seconds` is classified `operator_tunable`
   in `tests/restore-census.baseline.json`. Below 60 s the world tick charges every ticked character
   nothing, forever — no migration, no review, no guard. Today the tick's only channel is `gather`,
   so this is latent; it arms itself the day `combat` joins `channels`.

**Blast radius: the economy, not the character.** Vigour is the scarcity the 242,000-gold-a-day sink
is sold against (§4.4) and the 22-hour ceiling is the stated defence against "richest player hunts
most" becoming "richest player hunts always". Gold and loot from the uncharged windows are tradeable
on the market, so this is a faucet that crosses to other players' economies — the Target Property in
`CLAUDE.md` §1 — not a self-only cheat.

**Would an existing test have caught it?** No. `tests/vigour.mjs` drives the *SQL* (grant, ceiling,
refill ladder, replay) and the engine arms it runs feed one window at a time. Nothing anywhere asserts
that the charge is additive over a partition of a span. That guard now exists.

**Required change.** Make the charge conserve. Either:

- **(a) carry the remainder** — the `tool_carry` / `ammo_carry` shape `player_state` already uses for
  exactly this problem, as a `vigour_carry_ms int` written in the same delta; or
- **(b) charge in the ledger's own unit** — write `ev:vigour_ms` (or seconds) and let `hr_vigour_of`
  do the division, which removes the rounding from the hot path entirely.

(b) is cleaner and needs no new absolute key in `hr_apply`'s contract; (a) is a smaller diff. Either
way `vigourChargeMin` stops being the unit of account. **A floor on the window is NOT a fix** — the
`collect` exemption exists for a good reason (b531) and re-imposing a floor there would destroy real
paid windows, which is the defect the exemption was written to remove.

**Verification:** `node tests/vigour-charge-conservation.mjs` must exit 0, and the entry for it in
`tests/guards-unregistered.json` must be deleted and the plain run registered in
`.github/workflows/smoke.yml`. The guard is written to be the regression test for its own fix: its
`--selftest` plants a conserving charge in the engine and requires every arm to go green while the
shipped engine stays red, so the arms are proven to measure the law and not an implementation.

### S-2 — applying two of these files turns the nightly grant-hygiene detector red **[P1, BLOCKS the apply]**

`hr_assert_grant_hygiene(p_strict boolean default true)` collects, among nine checks:

```sql
-- 2026-09-21-engine-allowlist-tick-settle.sql:546-551
select … into v_engine_extra
  from pg_proc p …
 where n.nspname = 'public' and p.prokind in ('f','p')
   and has_function_privilege('hr_engine', p.oid, 'execute')
   and p.oid::regprocedure::text <> all (c_engine_allow);
…
if p_strict and (… or jsonb_array_length(v_engine_extra) > 0 …) then
  raise exception 'GRANT HYGIENE FAILED: %', v_report::text;
```

This lane grants `hr_engine` EXECUTE on two new functions:

- `vigour-daily.sql:145` — `grant execute on function public.hr_vigour_of(uuid, int) to hr_engine;`
- `hunt-analyzer.sql:239` — `grant execute on function public.hr_hunt_analyzer(uuid, int) to hr_engine;`

Neither appears in `c_engine_allow` anywhere in the repo. I checked both by name across every
migration and ran the derivation tool: `node tools/derive-grant-hygiene.mjs --check` → **exit 0**,
"derivation in sync (10 links, 11 patches)" — i.e. the allowlist as committed at this head does not
contain them and is not pending an edit that would.

**This is the posture both files claim to mirror, and the mirror is the proof.** `hr_bestiary_of`
is cited by name in both headers as the model. `hr_bestiary_of(uuid,integer)` needed its own
dedicated migration — `2026-08-20-live-progress-engine-allow.sql` — to be added to `c_engine_allow`,
and that file's §653-663 even asserts the entry took effect. Mirroring the grant without mirroring
the allowlist entry is half the pattern.

The detector runs at every apply **and nightly via `pg_cron`**, with failures surfacing as
`maintenance_alerts`. After these two applies it raises every night until someone notices.

**Required change.** One additional migration (or one anchored `do $$` block per file, following the
`2026-08-20-live-progress-engine-allow.sql` idiom) that re-derives `hr_assert_grant_hygiene` with two
new `c_engine_allow` entries, each with the one-line justification the array's own contract demands:

```
'hr_vigour_of(uuid,integer)',
-- read-only (STABLE, writes nothing, cannot be replayed into a gain). Two indexed point
-- reads on player_progress for ONE character plus hr_offline_cap_ms, which the engine
-- already holds. Its period keys are the server's own hr_utc_day_key(now()), never an
-- argument, so no call can widen the row set. NO NEW TARGET: p_user is the parameter the
-- engine already passes to hr_apply and hr_state_of.
'hr_hunt_analyzer(uuid,integer)',
-- read-only (STABLE) arithmetic over ONE character's own player_ledger rows since its own
-- active_since, priced from the sealed hr_items catalogue. Writes nothing, calls nothing
-- that writes. Same target argument: p_user is the parameter the engine already passes to
-- hr_state_of, whose envelope this is a narrower read of.
```

Do **not** fix this with `hr_grant_baseline_sync()` or by relaxing `p_strict`. Re-derive with
`tools/derive-grant-hygiene.mjs` and re-run `--check`.

**Pre-apply gate:** after the last of the four applies, run
`select public.hr_assert_grant_hygiene(true);` and require it to return without raising, **before**
the client half ships. That single statement would have caught this, and it is in the runbook in §4.

### S-3 — a ceiling-clamped refill charges full price and reports the full 120 minutes **[P1]**

`vigour-refill.sql:202` refuses only the case where the refill would buy **nothing**:

```sql
if coalesce((v_vig->>'budget_min')::int, 0) >= coalesce((v_vig->>'ceiling_min')::int, 0) then
  … return 'vigour_ceiling' …
```

The file's comment is explicit that this exists so "a player at a 22-hour grant would [not] pay 2,000
gold for air". It does not cover **partial** air. `hr_vigour_of` clamps with
`least(c_ceiling_min, v_grant + v_bought)` (`vigour-daily.sql:110`), so a refill that crosses the
ceiling delivers less than the 120 minutes it charges for — and `:254` reports the catalogue's
figure regardless:

```sql
v_result := jsonb_build_object('ok', true, 'nth', v_nth, 'cost', v_cost, 'minutes', v_min, …);
```

`v_min` is `hr_vigour_prices.minutes` = 120, unconditionally.

**Reachable, with whole-hour grants.** `offlineCapHours()` is 12 base plus renown perks plus property
tier (up to +4 at castle), so a 15-hour character has a 900-minute grant. After three refills the
budget is 1,260; the fourth is sold at **54,000 gold** and raises the budget to
`least(1320, 1380) = 1320` — **60 minutes delivered, 120 reported**. At a 16-hour grant (960) the
third refill delivers 120 and the ladder saturates; at 15 hours the fourth is half-price-for-full-fee
and the fifth would be pure air but is correctly refused by the same check *after* the fourth landed.

This is the `CLAUDE.md` §6 class inside a gold verb: the server takes real gold, tells the client
"120 minutes", and the meter on the same envelope says 60. The panel prints the receipt.

**Required change.** Compute the delivered minutes before the debit and either refuse or price
accordingly — refusing is the better answer and matches the file's own `already_owned` reasoning:

```sql
-- after v_cost/v_min are read, before the debit
if coalesce((v_vig->>'budget_min')::int,0) + v_min
     > coalesce((v_vig->>'ceiling_min')::int,0) then
  perform public.hr_record_rejection(v_uid, v_slot, 'vigour_refill', 'vigour_ceiling', …, 1);
  return jsonb_build_object('ok', false, 'error', 'vigour_ceiling', …);
end if;
```

If the Designer prefers to sell the partial, then `v_result` and the ledger `meta` must carry the
**delivered** minutes, not the catalogue's — a receipt that overstates what was bought is the defect
whatever the policy. Add a §7 arm on a perked probe character (the file already builds one for the
ceiling arms) that drives the ladder to the clamp and asserts the reported minutes equal the budget
delta.

### S-4 — `p_idem` is optional on a money verb **[P2]**

`vigour-refill.sql:278` — `create or replace function public.hr_vigour_refill(p_slot int default 0,
p_idem uuid default null)`. The defaults are justified in the header (PostgREST resolves by named
arguments; a two-argument function with no defaults answers PGRST202), and that reasoning is right
for `p_slot`. It is not right for `p_idem`, because the body treats a null key as "no idempotency at
all", on **both** ends:

- `:151` `hr_intent_replay` returns null immediately when `p_idem is null`, so nothing is looked up;
- `:261` `if p_idem is not null then insert into player_intents …`, so nothing is recorded.

A client that posts `{"p_slot":0}` therefore gets a verb that debits on every call. The per-day cap
bounds the loss at five rungs, so this is not a dupe and not a faucet — but it is a **real player
losing real gold to a double-tap or a retry on a flaky connection**, and the fix is one line. The
header's own §(9) comment ("the client generates a fresh key per gesture") is an assumption about a
client, which is exactly the kind of assumption a server verb must not hold.

**Required change.** Refuse a null key by name, before the advisory lock:

```sql
if p_idem is null then
  return jsonb_build_object('ok', false, 'error', 'missing_idem');
end if;
```

Keep the `default null` in the signature for the PostgREST reason; refuse it in the body. Add a §7
arm asserting a null-key call is refused and moves no gold, and add `missing_idem` to
`STATELESS_REFUSALS` if the client ever calls this verb.

### A-1 — the Analyzer runs five unbounded ledger passes on every envelope **[P2]**

`hunt-analyzer.sql:262` splices `'hunt_analyzer', public.hr_hunt_analyzer(p_user, v_st.slot),` into
`hr_state_of`, which is the envelope builder — it runs on every accrue poll, every intent, every
reload and (once combat is ticked) every world-tick settle for every rostered character.

The header's bound is not the bound that applies:

> "player_ledger_user_idx (user_id, slot, at desc) already exists, and a hunt is bounded below by
> `active_since` and above by `ACCRUE_MAX_SPAN_MS` (24 h) **per window**."

`ACCRUE_MAX_SPAN_MS` bounds **one window's length**, not the **number of windows** since
`active_since`. `active_since` moves only on `restart: true`. A character left on one monster and
settled at the ordinary cadence writes ~1,440 accrue rows a day, and `player_ledger`'s retention is
**90 days** — so the honest upper bound on the scan is ~130,000 rows, and there is no `LIMIT`, no
time floor and no early exit.

Over those rows the function makes **five separate passes** (`:146`, `:157`, `:171`, `:180`, `:186`),
two of which expand `jsonb_each_text` over `meta->'delta'->'i'` and `->'x'` and join `hr_items` /
`hr_skills`. That is five index scans plus two lateral expansions **per envelope**, for a block most
players will not have open.

It is correctly scoped — the `active_kind is distinct from 'combat'` early return at `:126` means
non-hunting characters pay nothing, and I confirm this is a **cost** finding, not a leak: `p_user` is
`hr_state_of`'s own parameter, every predicate is `user_id = p_user and slot = …`, the function is
`hr_engine`-only, and a forged call returns a different shape of the caller's own rows.

**Required change**, in the order I would do them:

1. **Fold passes 1–3 into one scan.** Passes 2 and 3 select the same rows as pass 1 with a different
   lateral; a single `select … from player_ledger l left join lateral (…) ` computes all of them in
   one pass. Pass 4 (deaths) has a different `intent` and pass 5 is `order by … limit 1`, which the
   index serves.
2. **Floor the lower bound.** `l.at > greatest(v_st.active_since, v_now - interval '24 hours')` is
   honest for a readout whose every rate is already per-hour, and it makes the scan bounded by the
   accrual cap the header already claims it is bounded by. If the Designer wants lifetime-of-hunt
   totals, they need a rollup, not a wider scan.
3. **Measure it.** `explain (analyze, buffers)` on a character with 50,000 accrue rows since
   `active_since`, before and after, in the post-apply verification of §4. A readout that costs more
   than the settle it describes is a readout that will be turned off under load.

There is no separate rate gate on this path and I do not think one is wanted: it inherits
`hr_state_of`'s callers, which are already gated. Making the query cheap is the right control.

### H-1 — `hr_hunt_stances`' knob columns are decorative **[P2]**

`hunt-stance-stop.sql:72-85` creates a three-column catalogue and seeds it, and the header says the
table exists because "'validated against a server catalogue' is the property the design asks for and
a CHECK list is a second copy of `src/core/hunt.js` STANCES that no query can compare against".

The **validation** genuinely uses it — `hr_apply`'s patched arm does
`not exists (select 1 from hr_hunt_stances where stance_id = v_act->>'stance')`, which is the right
authority and I verified it is the only stance check that cannot be bypassed by a compromised edge.

The **values** are read by nothing. The engine resolves a stance through `stanceOf(inp.huntStance)`
(`accrual.js:1789`), which reads the frozen JS `STANCES` table. `eat_at`, `ammo_dry` and `falls` in
SQL are never selected by any function, any RPC or any client — I searched the repo and
`src/render/hunt-panel.js` does not read the table either (it renders `STANCE_WORDS`, a local label
map, and the server-projected `hunt_stance` string).

So: an operator who tunes `hr_hunt_stances.eat_at` changes nothing and is told nothing. That is the
"two places that can disagree" shape the design spent §9 rejecting, arriving by the other door — and
it is worse than a plain duplicate because one copy is silently inert. `tests/hunt-stance-stop.mjs`
S2 compares the two by regex over the **migration text**, which keeps the seed honest but does not
make the table load-bearing.

**Required change** — pick one and say which in the file:

- **(a)** Say so in the table comment and in the header: the catalogue is an **id allowlist**; the
  knob columns are documentation of what the engine does, are not read at runtime, and a fourth
  column is still a finding. Keep S2. Cheapest, and honest.
- **(b)** Make the engine read them — pass the row through the envelope beside `hunt_stance` and let
  `stanceOf` take values rather than look them up. This is the version that makes the table mean what
  the header says, and it is a bigger change than this lane should take on today.

I do not block on this, but (a) must land: a catalogue that claims to be authority and is not will be
tuned by somebody eventually.

### H-2 — the stop-shape CHECK can raise instead of returning false **[P3, defence-in-depth]**

`hr_hunt_stop_valid` (`hunt-stance-stop.sql:113-127`) tests the digits and then casts:

```sql
when (p_stop ? 'hours') and not (jsonb_typeof(p_stop->'hours') = 'number'
      and (p_stop->>'hours') ~ '^\d+$'
      and (p_stop->>'hours')::bigint between 1 and 24) then false
```

`jsonb` numbers are `numeric` and hold far more than a `bigint`. `{"hours": 1e30}` passes
`jsonb_typeof = 'number'`, renders as 31 digits, matches `^\d+$`, and then overflows the cast —
raising `22003` from inside a CHECK constraint rather than returning `false`.

**Unreachable today**, and I want to be precise about why rather than inflate it: `hr_apply` is
engine-only, the edge's `validateStop` refuses anything that is not `Number.isInteger` and in range
(`src/core/hunt.js:validateStop`), and `hr_apply`'s own patched arm calls this function and turns a
`false` into a named `bad_stop` refusal first. So the only way in is a caller that reaches `hr_apply`
without the edge — which is the case a re-validation layer exists for.

**Required change.** Cast to `numeric`, which cannot overflow here, or bound the length first:

```sql
and (p_stop->>'hours')::numeric between 1 and 24
```

Same for `food_floor`, `ammo_floor`, `falls`. One arm in §6 that feeds `{"hours": 1e30}` and requires
`false` rather than a raise.

### C-1 — the client is clean, and the one thing it renders is wrong **[P2]**

I reviewed `src/render/hunt-panel.js`, `src/net/accrue.js` (`hydrateHunt`) and `src/net/activity.js`
against `CLAUDE.md` §6 and found **nothing client-authored**:

- every value lands in `_`-prefixed scratch (`G._hunt`, `G._vigour`, `G._huntAnalyzer`), which is
  never persisted and absent on a cold boot;
- nothing is in `RESIDUE_FIELDS`, nothing is merged upward, and `hunt_analyzer: null` **replaces**
  the previous reading rather than being coalesced, which is the right answer to the phantom-seed
  class;
- `hydrateHunt` reads by `hasOwnProperty`, so "this build has no hunts" and "no stance chosen" stay
  distinguishable and an older deployment renders no panel rather than a fabricated one;
- there is no refill button (design §6 ships Vigour read-only) and no client-side kill counter;
- the one wall-clock value is the elapsed pill, which nothing gates on and nothing can spend.

The naming call — `hydrateHunt`, not `reconcileHunt`, because there is nothing to reconcile — is
correct and I would have raised it the other way round.
