**The required change to A: a partied row was exempt, and that reopened F1's party mint through the client-direct RPCs.** `hr_settle_first_of` returned `null` for any row in a live party hunt (lane line 171), on the premise that "a partied member's window is owned and priced per window by `hr_party_tick_settle`". F1's review already showed that premise is false today. The party channel is SHADOW, `hr_party_tick_settle` moves no member watermark, and the member's own settle pays the whole hunt **after the stop, at the state that exists then** (`2026-09-24-m8-parties-s4-2-hunt-intents.sql:11-17`). `hr_party_hunt_start` is granted to `authenticated` and sets `active_kind = 'combat'` (payable). Under the lane as written, this sequence works: start a hunt, idle overnight, call `hr_set_auto_eat`, `hr_trait_buy`, `hr_claim_*` or `hr_credit_kills`, stop, then accrue. The whole hunt is priced at the new state. The mutant proves it on the replayed chain: with the exemption restored, an `hr_apply items:+1` on a 7.25 h-stale partied row **commits** (bag 1→2, version 2→3, 2 ledger rows).

**The fix** (`settle-before-mutate.sql:163-183, 198, 292`):
- A stale, payable, partied row is refused `party_hunt_running`. This is the code and posture F1's edge uses (`party-fence.js PARTY_CHANNEL_PAYS = false`). The new constant `c_party_channel_pays` flips in the same commit that arms S5.
- `hr_require_settled` raises the body's own code, and `hr_apply` releases the intent key for `party_hunt_running`, as it does for `settle_first`.
- The refusal can be cleared: stopping the hunt drops the row back to `settle_first`, and the member's own accrue then clears that.
- New tests: an executed §4(b) arm; guard **R6** in `tests/settle-before-mutate.mjs`; mutant **`partyExempt`** (RED ×7). `--selftest` passes with all 3 mutants caught.

## Findings

| # | Question | Finding | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1a | Which rows `hr_require_settled` refuses | A row is refused `settle_first` when `active_kind ∈ {combat, gather, artisan}` AND `now() − accrued_to > 180 s`. Rows are not refused when idle, when there is no character or when `accrued_to` is null. A partied row that meets both conditions is now refused `party_hunt_running`. **The 180 s threshold vs F1's 60 s floor:** F1's edge settles any window over 60 s before its verb, so the gap between 60 and 180 s never refuses an edge verb. The DB is the backstop for the client-direct RPCs, with ≤180 s of accepted residue (the same threshold as combat-XP, pinned by S2). **The offline cap:** a capped settle stamps `now`, not the paid instant, so a 20 h absence clears on its first accrue. | `settle-before-mutate.sql:160-189`; `accrual.js:2845-2851, 3367` |
| 1b | Can a client be wedged? | **No permanent wedge.** After a paid settle the watermark sits within one action of `now`. The largest action in `src/data` is 14 s, far below 180 s (`settledWatermarkMs` returns `now` for capped, collect and remainder ≥ step, `accrual.js:3367-3373`). An unpriceable pointer is released by the existing forfeit on stop (`set-activity.js:594-608`), and a partied row by the stop. **The refusal body does not tell the client to settle.** It carries `{error:'settle_first', unsettled_ms, threshold_ms, slot}`, and only the combat-XP flush handles it (`src/legacy.js:4295`). A claim, bank move, harvest, auto-eat toggle or trait buy refused this way shows a generic error until the next attended accrue (≤90 s), then succeeds on a retry. **P2 client half, not blocking:** it fails closed, writes nothing and heals itself. It should ride the same cut: route `settle_first` / `party_hunt_running` from the client-direct callers into `awaitSettleRaceClear` plus one retry, and latch claim/shop controls until the boot settle answers (verdict §F2 UX note). | `settle-before-mutate.sql:172-183`; `src/net/accrue.js:596-616` |

It is not a mint. Game-designer may still want to rule on it (P3). **`hr_apply`** backstops every engine path. | sweep script over `bootReplay()` (this review); bodies at chain end |
