# 6. Gear

Slots, tiers, armor lines, and the five parts a piece of gear can have: Base, Trait, Signature, Temper and Imbuement. Plus carry-over, jewelry, named pieces and the old layers that fold away.

---

## 6.1 Gear drops, it isn't made
**[Unchanged]**

- Armor, weapons and shields **drop from monsters**. A bench never makes them.
- Every piece has a **home**: a ground and a monster you can learn. Chasing a piece is a plan, not only luck.
- A bench can only remake a few named pieces (Klappa's Klub, the Steelgrate).
- Crafters make the rest of the kit: capes, jewelry, ammunition, runes, food and Tempering.
- **Gear never breaks.** Only tools wear out, and that's a later patch (chapter 9).

## 6.6 Who can wear what
**[Unchanged]**

- Gear is gated by **hero Level only**.
- A piece counts for at most about half a tier above your Level. Wearing far ahead doesn't help more.
- Plain upgrades that are better in every way are worn by themselves, for all four heroes. In 0.3 a hero can also swap a plain upgrade mid-hunt at a safe moment.

Tyler dropped a plan to scale gear with Level (2026-11-22): "I don't want to punish people who are theory crafting or getting lucky with drops."

## 6.7 The five parts of a piece
**[New in 0.3]**

| Part | Where it comes from | Random? | Its job |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Base** | Drops, with a home | No. Fixed by type and tier | The floor. A plain upgrade is better in every way |
| **Trait** | Rolled when a Rare drops | Yes. Which Trait and rank I to III | A rule that suits some heroes and grounds. This is the decision |
| **Signature** | Hand-made, on a few named pieces | No | Changes how one move works. This is the story |
| **Temper** | A smith | No | The steady upgrade. Stays with the hero's slot |
| **Imbuement** | Made from monster parts, lasts hunted hours | No | The bit you change for the ground |

## 6.9 Traits
**[New in 0.3]**

A Trait is a rule, written in the same words as the action bar.

- Examples: "+2.5% area damage when hitting 3+ enemies". "−1.5% damage taken below 40% health". "+3.5% healing while 2+ party members are below 50% health".
- **Rank I, II or III.** The tooltip shows about 1.5%, 2.5% and 3.5% for ranks I to III. A Trait with a cost shows a bigger gain.
- **Every Trait of a rank has the same budget** on the ground and seat that suit it best. No Trait is meant to be the top pick everywhere.
- **Traits answer roles.** The best Trait changes with the ground.
- **No stacking.** Traits with the same key on one hero count the larger. A party-wide Trait counts once per party.
- **Some Traits are trades,** a gain with a cost. Hask's Bargain hits harder above 90% health and takes more damage there.
- **No reroll for gold.** Trading and carry-over are how you fix a bad roll.
- **Where a Trait rolls is knowledge.** Each ground has a Trait pool, and the bestiary shows it once the page is far enough along.

**Ranks by depth and by ground:**
- Depth opens ranks: rank I from depth 1, rank II from depth 8, rank III from depth 15.
- The ground caps them by its entry Level: rank I from Level 40 grounds, rank II from Level 70, rank III from Level 100.
- So Muster Barrows (entry 55) rolls rank I only. Pyre Field and Krell's Hold reach rank II. The Catacombs reach rank III.

**Traits in 0.3: 15 of 40.** They roll on open grounds only.

| Ground | Traits |
|---|---|
| Muster Barrows (6) | File Breaker, Last File, First Rank, Captain's Due, Banded Oath, Hask's Bargain |
| Pyre Field (4) | Worrier, Pyre Tender, Scorched Grip, Long Rite |
| Krell's Hold (5) | Ram's Head, Bailey Stand, Tarred Through, Raised Colors, Lookout's Promise |

The other 25 roll when their grounds open (Carter's Rest, the Ox Road, Wyvern Roost, the Catacombs). Hask's Ford and the Slake have no pool. All 40 are in chapter 15.

Tyler picked all 40 candidates: "Sadly I like them all."

Not final: Traits are hidden data today. The roll, the drop moment and the screens are lane T1, the biggest new server piece in 0.3. The first Trait a player meets is at Level 55.

## 6.10 The drop moment
**[New in 0.3]**

- A Trait piece gets its own line on the return card.
- A Rare reveal plays on every Rare drop.
- A Signature drop gets a reveal, a line in town, a mark on your house, and a card you can share.
- A Rare tooltip says who found it, where and how deep: "Found by Rook · Krell's Hold · depth 14".

## 6.11 Signatures
**[New in 0.3]**

A Signature is a named piece with one fixed rule nothing else gives. It changes how one move plays.

- Examples: **Pikeman's Gauntlets**: Flame Strike makes Power Strike ready again. **Cairn Belt**: below 30% health, gain a shield for 25% of max health (60 s cooldown). **Hall Fire Wand**: Fireball on a kill makes the monster explode for 30% of its max health to every monster next to it.
- **Rarer than any Very Rare.** About 30 to 60 hunted hours of farming its home for one given Signature.
- **One home each.** A boss, a named monster on a deep ground, or the Tower's Hearth Chest.
- **One per hero counts.** A second can be worn but only the first one equipped works.
- **No stacking with itself.** Two party members with the same aura count once.
- A Signature works with every branch of its move.
- A free cast or reset from a Signature can't set off another one.
- Signatures don't read the flame. Your luck never changes how often one drops.
- **They trade,** as single pieces, and every Signature sale is told in town (when the market ships).

**In 0.3: 23 Signatures**, from Muster Barrows, Hask's Ford, the Slake, Pyre Field, Krell's Hold and the Tower's Hearth Chest. Five are built and played first as a sample: Bannerman's Pavise, Pikeman's Gauntlets, Barrow Knight's Blade, Bargeman's Pole and Cairn Belt.

**Out of 0.3:** Tollman's Gloves and Hearth Mason's Cloak (they change drops and need the market), Raft Builder's Earrings (its move isn't live), and every Signature whose home is a staged ground. The support's 8 come with the wraps. All 46 are in chapter 15.

## 6.12 Temper
**[Changed]**

**In 0.3:**
- The smith tempers a **gear slot**, not a piece. Temper 3 on your weapon slot is +3 item Levels on whatever weapon you wear.
- **Five ranks per slot.** A new rank opens every 40 Levels: rank 1 at Level 40, 2 at 80, 3 at 120, 4 at 160, 5 at 200.
- **When you wear a new tier,** your ranks come with you but sit "unseated" until you pay to re-seat them, at about a third of their price. Your plan does it at the next town stop up to a price you set ("Re-seat Temper · up to N gold"), so you never press it.
- **Sell or swap a piece and its Temper stays with your hero.** Never lost, not to a death, a sale or a swap.
- A rank costs Temper Kits (made by Smithing) plus a gold fee that's burned. Half and half.
- A whole kit at rank 5 is worth about one Level's worth of a tier, small and steady. A new tier always beats Temper.

| Example at Rune tier (item Level 60) | Gold |
|---|---|
| Weapon slot, rank 1 | about 8,500 |
| Whole kit, rank 1 (Level 40 to 79) | about 42,500 |
| Whole kit, all five ranks, at item Level 200 | about 1.1 million |

The slot tooltip reads "Temper 2 / 5 · Next rank at Level 120".

**Today (going away in 0.3):** five 1% rungs per piece on two tracks (blows and armor), paid in gold and Tempering Salts by tier, from 2,000 gold for the first Iron rung to 640,000 for the last Dawnsteel rung.

Not final: the Temper Kit recipe and the exact prices. Lane TP builds it.

## 6.13 Carry-over
**[New in 0.3]**

Outgrown a great Rare? An artificer moves its Traits onto a new plain piece.

- **What moves:** every Trait on the old piece, with its rank. One on a Rare, both on a Very Rare (at twice the price).
- **Where to:** a plain piece of the same slot and the same or a higher tier. A ring Trait fits either ring.
- **The old piece is destroyed.**
- **What it takes:** rare parts from the Trait's home ground (2 + its rank, per Trait), a gold fee that's burned, and 1 work-hour from an Artificing hero.
- **Nothing rolls.** No failure, no reroll.
- Not allowed onto a lower tier, for a Signature, or while a piece is listed on the market.
- It's one press each time on purpose, because it destroys a piece. It happens about once a tier per slot.

| Example, onto item Level 80 | Parts | Gold |
|---|---|---|
| File Breaker rank I | 3 Muster parts | about 35,500 |
| Captain's Due rank II | 4 | about 62,000 |
| A Very Rare, two rank III Traits | 10 | about 177,000 |

## 6.14 Imbuements
**[Changed]**

**In 0.3:**
- A hero carries three: an **Edge** on the weapon (offense), a **Ward** on the body (defense), a **Leech** on the helmet (sustain).
- Each answers something a ground does: its brutes, its fire, its long fights.
- An **artificer makes them** from a rare part found on another ground, plus remains. So a part from one ground is wanted on another.
- Each lasts **20 hunted hours** while worn (Fine 25, Master 30). Time in town costs nothing.
- Applying one costs a small gold fee, burned. A new one on a slot replaces the old one, with a warning.
- They never key on a monster family. They key on a role, an element or an effect.
- Your plan keeps them up per ground: "Krell's Hold · Body: Fire Ward · Keep imbued".

**The first five:**

| Imbuement | Tooltip | Slot | Made from | Best on |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fire Ward | −8% Fire damage taken | Body | Fire Gland (Wyvern Roost) | Krell's Hold, front hero |
| Fire Edge | 5% of damage dealt as Fire | Weapon | Coal Tongs (Carter's Rest) | The Ox Road |
| Brute Edge | +3% damage vs Brutes | Weapon | Pike Ferrule (Pyre Field) | Muster Barrows, melee |
| Life Leech | Life Leech 1% | Helmet | Leech Jaw (Unmarked Catacombs) | Wyvern Roost, melee front |
| Mana Leech | Mana Leech 1% | Helmet | Urn Shard (Muster Barrows) | Pyre Field, mage |

Imbuements share a small cap with draughts and the camp pot (chapter 8). The hero card shows "Consumables 6 / 6".

**Today (going away in 0.3):** five kinds (Keen, Life Leech, Mana Leech, Ward, Element Ward) at three tier floors (Levels 1, 30, 80).

Not final: four of the five sample parts come from grounds that aren't open yet, so in 0.3 they come from the Tower, other players or later grounds. Lane IM builds it.

## 6.19 How big gear can get
**[New in 0.3]**

- One gear tier is a big step. It moves how deep a party holds by one to three depths.
- All Traits and the Signature on a hero together count for at most about one tier.
- So a lucky kit is never more than about one tier ahead of an unlucky one at the same Level.
- The next tier with no luck still beats the last tier with full luck, on average.
