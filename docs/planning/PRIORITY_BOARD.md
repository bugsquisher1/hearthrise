**A. Hunt danger**

*What the engine does today (measured, not read).*
1. **Food on its 2 s cooldown is a sustain line.** While a pull's damage per 2 s stays below what one meal restores, food absorbs all of it and the only cost is gold. Once a pull crosses that line, the hero dies within minutes. Damage never kills slowly.
2. **Every in-band default pull sits far below the line.** No hero died in any of about 40 rows. That includes 6 levels under Mosslight with the largest pull, even eating at 35% instead of 60% (52 close calls an hour, still no deaths). This matches the vitals.
3. **In a party the back line is never hit.** Ranger, Mage and Healer took 0 blows in every party run, because the tank's Challenge holds everything (ruling 10-16).
4. **Single blows are small:** at most 8.5-13.5% of a floor hero's HP in heavy armour, and 19-27% in cloth or leather. The worst is a Thicket wolf's blow on a Level-5 Ranger.
5. **The "spikes" are lockstep.** Every monster swings the moment it arrives and then every 2.0 s, so a pack that arrives together hits together. At the largest pull, 72-79% of blows land in the same 100 ms step as another blow (worst single instant on today's data: 42% of HP). At a pull of 4 that share is 6%.

*The curve (designer ruling).* Solo, own-tier set, the ground's starting pull. "Low point" is the lowest HP in a pull, as median / 10th percentile. A close call is HP under 30%.

**B. The Level-32 wall**

*Evidence (real settle, full own-tier sets, per hero per hour).* Coppercap Warrens alone: Level 32 nets -8,824 gold (-77%), 36 nets -4,449, 40 nets +2,113 (break-even, -7% to +19%), 45 nets +6,811 and buys no food. Two of your own heroes at 32: tank + healer +4,282 (+434% to +902%), ranger + healer +5,165, melee + healer +17% to +23%, tank + mage -7% to +6%, tank + ranger -17% to -28%. In the Sewer every own pair clears +200%. Staying in Mosslight at 38 pays 71k Level XP an hour against 130-170k in the Warrens. On the real XP curve, Level 31 to 50 is 6.77M Level XP, about 40-48 hunting hours at Warrens rates, so all of Greenhaven (1-50) is roughly 70-80 hunting hours for a dedicated player (unverified total: today's grounds' rates).

*Ruling: both, along Tyler's own ladder* (his Greenhaven Frontier list of 10-15, workbook at `docs/design/world-workbook/Hunting_Grounds.csv`: three solo grounds and two party grounds up to 50, then Thornwild Expanse 40-120).
1. **Next ground: H-004 Pebblebrook Bank, solo, band 28-45.** The workbook says 23-42; it moves so that it overlaps Mosslight's top and reaches the Level where a lone hero can take the Warrens. The workbook's creatures are Mudskipper, River Snapper, Marsh Lizard, Forest Spider and Sporeling (families: aquatic, fungal, insectoid, reptilian). The five are the ground lane's sample. "Forest Spider" is renamed for the place (e.g. "Shingle Spider", which hunts the pebble shingle), and the region's naming voice (brook, mill, ford, croft) goes in the data header. Loot: the tier-3 set in all three weights as Scarce rows (Steel, Boarhide and Scholar, about one an hour per slot per weight: Tyler 10-23), tier-3 caster and bow weapons as Rares, and tier-4 helmets on its rare carrier (as Rook does for tier 3). It starts its pull at 4 against the curve above. Its creatures and map need paid art, which needs Tyler's fresh approval per spend (CLAUDE.md §2).
2. **Party grounds are built for two of your own heroes.** Every account starts with two hero slots. Standing rule: at a party ground's floor, an own pair **with a healer** clears at least +25%, and the card says "Best with a healer." Today the Sewer passes for every pair and the Warrens for every healer pair. Melee + healer sits at +17% to +23% in the harness, which wears the Melee in cloth; lane D2 re-measures it in plate. A pair without a healer in the Warrens is a waste hunt, and the card says so. Warrens `soloFrom` stays 45.
3. **Then H-006, the workbook's "Forgotten Rootcellar"** (party, 40-50, carrying the tier-4 set). Its "Forgotten" is a banned filler word; the proposed name is "Hollins Croft Rootcellar" (a founder's surname plus croft, both in the Greenhaven voice), for the ground lane to confirm. H-002 Old Mill Road (solo, 6-25) is not needed for the wall (the Thicket, the Sewer and Mosslight cover 1-31) and comes last; its "Shadowfang Wolf" is renamed (banned word).
4. **The ladder above 50.** One region equals six grounds, three solo and three party, with bands overlapping by at least 5 levels (the workbook's shape). Standing rule: **every 10-level step has at least one solo ground in band and one party ground in band**, and a party ground counts for a lone hero only from its `soloFrom`. Thornwild as written has solo grounds up to 92 and party grounds only from 76, and nothing solo from 92 to 120. So its first build is H-007 Briarfen Marsh (solo 40-62) plus H-010 Fungalfall Hollow re-banded to 55-80 as its first party ground, and H-011 Blackroot Burrows gets a `soloFrom`. Thornwild should be ready within about two weeks of Early Access opening, since a dedicated player clears Greenhaven in roughly a week.

Defense 19 to 30 (Fingerguns' wall) is 247,860 XP: 7.6-22.5 h for a tank, and 128-210 h of solo hunting for a Healer, Ranger or Mage, never in a party. The gate asks casters and rangers to do the one thing their role exists to avoid.

*Ruling.* **Light (cloth) and medium (leather) armour need the hero's Level at the tier's level (Level 15 / 30 / 45 / 60 / 75 / 88). Heavy (plate) armour and shields keep Defense. Weapons keep their own style skill.**
- Level rises from every kill in every role (party Level XP is split evenly, 10-22), so a Mage at Level 30 wears Scholar cloth and a Ranger at Level 30 wears Boarhide.
- Plate stays the armour of whoever stands in front and gets hit, so its gate still means something, and it is the same rule as "the shield makes the tank" (10-23).
- Mix-and-match survives: a Melee who wants cloth's damage needs Level, not Magic.
- Defense stays worth training for everyone, since each Defense level lowers a monster's chance to hit by 2 points (`combat.js`).
- Fingerguns' Steel Helm stays a plate piece (Defense 30, and it costs a Mage 5 magic accuracy); his own tier-3 hat, the Scholar Hat, opens at Level 30.

*Not chosen.* A shared party Defense contradicts Tyler 10-22 ("only blows taken train Defense") and would let a back-liner wear plate without ever being hit. Gating cloth on Magic and leather on Ranged breaks mix-and-match. Defense XP from nearby blows or from heals adds a second rule to one skill.

*Flag for the Coordinator:* `gear-tiers.js` carries a pre-rebuild note, "ARMOUR REQUIREMENTS ARE DEFENCE-ONLY, PERMANENTLY" (Tyler 2026-08-15). It is not in the rulings of record. This ruling keeps that note's reason (any hero can wear any weight, never gated on Magic or Ranged) and replaces its mechanism for light and medium armour. Confirm with Tyler if he holds 08-15 as live.

*Second gap found.* Tier-3 cloth and leather drop only in the Warrens (plus Rook's 0.38% tier-3 helmet in Mosslight). Tier-4 cloth and leather, the Willow-tier and Maple-tier wands, staffs, spellbooks and bows have no source at all except the Warrens' Rares (Chief Coppercap 1.25%, Goblin Skulker 0.62%). No gloves or belts beyond tier 1 exist anywhere. Pebblebrook Bank (B1), the Rootcellar (B3) and the style-gear rung 2 and 3 batches (Crafting/Smithing 30 and 45, already planned on 10-22) close this.

**Marketplace design (2026-10-24)** (game-designer, inside Tyler's 10-24 objective: "as convenient as a modern trading application while retaining the atmosphere of a classic fantasy MMORPG" and "no duping or exploiting can occur"; lane/d-market-design, design only, no code). **0. Stacks, not single pieces (the answer first).** Every copy of an item id is identical, so a listing is an item id and a count and a buyer can take any part of it. Evidence: the bag is `player_inventory (user_id, slot, item_id) → qty` (`2026-08-11-player-state.sql`), worn gear is `player_equipment (user_id, slot, equip_slot) → item_id` (equipping debits one unit), Storage is `account_bank (user_id, item_id) → qty` (`2026-11-27-account-depot.sql`), a hunt's packed supplies are `hunt_bag (session_id, item_id) → qty` (`2026-10-21-hunt-sessions.sql`), and a listing is `market_listings.item_id + qty`. No table has a wear, durability, roll or per-piece column. Gear stats are fixed (Tyler 10-14). Imbuing is not built (`src/data/imbuements.js`: "no server table"). The one upgrade that exists, the weapon enchant, is `player_state.enchant {equip_slot: element}`: it sits on the hero's worn slot, not on the item. **Rule for later lanes:** any per-piece state (the L4 imbuement) lives on the worn slot, and an imbued piece cannot be taken off until its imbuement runs out or is removed. That is Tyler's 10-15 "untradable until expiry or paid removal", and it means a stack never holds a copy that differs from the rest.

**1. Purpose test.**
- **Problem it solves.** Surplus has one outlet today: the shop. On 10-23, one account's four heroes held 59 Iron Platelegs in their bags (28, 15, 10 and 6); the shop pays 142 each (0.5 of book 285; tier-3 gear gets 0.14). Gear drops about once an hour per slot per weight (Tyler 10-23), so most of it is surplus to the hero who finds it and an upgrade for someone else. Materials move more volume. By book value the biggest holdings are Normal Logs (22,995), Steel Whetstones (1,340), Raw Silverfin, Cooked Shrimp (4,472), Raw Shrimp, Coal and Oak Planks. Example: the shop pays 9 for a Cooked Shrimp (heals 96). A hunter without one pays 14 per use for the store's Salted Shrimp (heals 72), so any price from 10 to 18 leaves both players better off. At 13 the cook gets 12.48 after the cut (+39% over the shop), and the hunter pays 0.135 g per HP instead of the store's 0.194 (-30%).
- **Decisions it adds.** At every drop: keep it, store it, sell it at the shop, or list it (and at what price). Farm or buy: an hour of Mosslight for the platelegs, or 240 gold now. Which ground to farm, because the market value of a ground's drops becomes another axis grounds compete on (DESIGN_STANDARDS §5). Whether to craft for sale.
- **What it plugs into.** Loot bands and loot rules, Storage (per account), the account purse, crafting inputs and outputs, and housing costs: the War Crown is Chief Coppercap's rare (11 found so far), and 4 of them are part of the Shrine's last rung, The Ossuary; planks and bars build the storage chests. Also hunt supplies, the Bestiary (where each item drops) and gold history (10-23).
- **Gold sink.** A 1% listing fee plus the Weighhouse's 4% cut of each sale, both destroyed. Over the last 14 days about 785k gold came in and about 150k went out (unverified split: 512k of the inflow predates `gold_kind` stamping). Market gold is a transfer, never minted, so the sink grows with trade and leaves the loot model's mint caps alone.
- **Hour 100 and hour 1,000.** Yes: Trophies (~140 h), War Crowns, next-tier gear in every zone, housing materials. It is depth rather than extra complexity: one window with four tabs.

**2. What the player sees.**
- **The place.** The Weighhouse on the Common's square, beside the well, kept by the weighmaster Aldous Pell. Both names were checked: unused in the repo and free of banned words. A weighhouse is the real town building where goods were weighed for trade. The fiction explains the rules: the Weighhouse holds what you list, pays your purse the moment it sells, and carries unsold goods back to your storage. Tapping the building or Aldous opens the Market (new `TAP_OPENS` rows plus a map object in `town_common.js`).
- **How far convenience reaches: everywhere.** The icon row has a Market button, and the market works fully for every hero, hunting or not. It is a window on desktop and a slide-out sheet on landscape phones. Why: heroes hunt for hours on the server, and the market moves account goods (Storage, the purse) that no single hero carries. Walking to the same building on every visit repeats a decision already made, which breaks the first principle. The atmosphere comes from the place, the players seen there in town, the weighmaster's line and the ledger look, not from a walk. *Handoff:* the Storage window's "Leave the hunt to use it" is client-only (`hr_bank_move` has no hunt check) and conflicts with this and with Quick stash (10-23). Systems and the Art Director should revisit it.
- **Browse.** One row per ITEM, not per listing: icon, name, tier and weight, "from 13 each", "320 for sale", "last sold 13, 2 h ago". The categories are the bag's (`bag-model.js` BAG_CATS: All, Gear, Food, Drinks, Ammo & runes, Loot, Materials, Rare, Other), with Gear split by slot and weight. Search covers every tradeable item, whether listed or not ("No one is selling Coal. Last sold 2 days ago for 12 each."). Filters: Usable by <hero> (meets the requirement), armour weight, slot, tier, For sale now. Sort: lowest price (the default), most for sale, recently sold, name. The account remembers all of these.
- **Item page.** What the item is for and where it drops or how it is made (the Bestiary and recipe rows). "For sale" grouped by price ("13 each · 180", "14 each · 60"), a 30-day line of the daily median price with volume, and the last 10 sales (count, price each, time ago). Your own listings show "Yours" with Cancel.
- **Price discovery.** Every row shows the lowest ask, how many are for sale, the last sale and the 7-day median. The bag, Storage and loot-rule tooltips gain one line under "The shop pays 9 each" (`item-tip.js`): "Market: from 13 · last sold 13". History stays honest because same-account buys are refused and same-network sales are left out of it (§3, §4).
- **Buying.** Pick a quantity (1, 10, 100, Most I can afford, All) and it fills cheapest first across listings: "Buy 120 for 1,560 gold". The delivery choice, "Goes to: Storage" (default) or "Goes to: <hero>'s bag", is remembered. The sheet shows the purse after the purchase and warns if a running hunt's "Keep at least" gold floor would be crossed. One press buys. In v1 the client buys the listings in the confirmation one by one. If one sells first it stops and says so ("Bought 100. The last 20 at 13 sold first. Buy 20 at 14?"), and it never pays above the confirmed price; listing prices cannot change after posting.
- **Selling.** Items can be listed from the bag or from Storage: "List on the market…" in their menus, or the Sell tab, which shows the viewed hero's bag and Storage with "Market: from X" and "The shop pays Y" on each row. The sell sheet has a quantity (All, half, custom) and a price each, with chips: Lowest now, Last sold, Your last price, and Suggested (the catalogue value, used when there is no market data). Before pressing, the player sees: "Listing fee: 8 gold now (kept if it doesn't sell). When one sells, the Weighhouse keeps 4%: you get 230 each. Listed for 7 days; anything unsold comes back to your storage."
- **Buy orders in v1: no.** A buy order holds gold out of the purse, and running hunts spend from that purse every time they use a store supply (store bills, the gold_out end, the "Keep at least" floor). That needs its own rule ("held for buy orders"; hunts spend only the free part of the purse) and its own matching surface, so it is a later lane.
- **Length, cancel, expiry.** One length, 7 days; the fee doesn't depend on it, so there is no choice to make. Cancel any time: the goods go to Storage at once and the fee is kept. At expiry the goods go back to Storage automatically.
- **Sale notices.** Online: a toast and a System chat line per sale, batched per minute, following the `alerts.js` pattern used for Tamsin's tally payments. Away: one line in the away recap. Gold history lists each sale (kind `trade`).
- **My listings and History.** My listings shows each listing as "2 of 3 left · 240 each · 6 days left · received 230" with Cancel. History covers 30 days of sales, purchases, returns and fees, with "List again" on anything that came back, "List all again" (same prices) and "Buy again". These, the remembered price chip and the remembered delivery choice cover every decision a seller or buyer would otherwise repeat.
- **Anonymous.** No seller or buyer name appears anywhere in the market. It is an exchange, not a stall: names invite harassment, price griefing and real-money-trade coordination. Named, social trading is right-click Trade, its own later lane.

**3. Rules and numbers** (every number is an `hr_market_config` value, PROVISIONAL, and gets re-tuned with Tyler's economy sims; the per-unit rules hold for any number).

| Rule | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Who owns a listing | the ACCOUNT; the listing hero is recorded for "listed by" in My listings only | gold is per account (10-23) and unsold goods return to the account's Storage; any hero of the account can cancel |
| Sources | the viewed hero's bag, or Storage | Tyler's brief; both are server rows the market debits under their own lock |
| Returns (cancel, expiry, item made unlistable) | always into Storage, never refused | one destination, and it never fills a hunting hero's bag |
| Storage room | room used = kinds in Storage + kinds the account has listed (one function, read by `hr_bank_move` and the market) | otherwise listing 1 unit at 1 gold of fee and letting it return would open Storage stacks past capacity, skipping the storage chests (a 1,500 to 90,000 g sink). Returns always fit, and nothing ever goes over the cap |
| Delivery | Storage (default) or the buying hero's bag; refused if full | the buyer is present and can choose again |
| Listing fee | 1% of qty x ask, rounded up, at least 1; paid at listing, kept on cancel and expiry | a sink; makes cancel-and-relist undercut wars cost something; no refund path to exploit |
| Weighhouse cut | 4% of each sale, rounded up (`house_tax_bp` 150 → 400), out of the seller's proceeds | the sink. 5% in all is low enough that listing beats arranging a trade off the market (judgement, unverified) |
| Price floor | at least the shop's bid for one (`hr_hunt_vendor_value.unit`: all 508 listable items have one, and none is above book) | no listing asks less than the shop pays, so buying a listing to sell it to the shop can never profit, and a "gift" listing can move at most book minus the shop's bid (zero for a War Crown, whose bid is its book) |
| Price ceiling | at most 100 x book value per unit, never under 1,000 | stops absurd asks and fat fingers; the top-book item (270,000) can still ask 27M; makes laundering through junk need bulk volume |
| Open listings | 50 per account | covers a four-hero account's surplus |
| New listings | 200 per account per UTC day | the old per-slot churn cap, moved to the account |
| Length | 7 days, one length | an idle player away for days still sells |
| Daily caps | 2,000,000 spent and 2,000,000 received per account per UTC day | 2 to 4.5 days of a four-hero account hunting around the clock at Mosslight's measured rate (coins 4.6k per hero-hour; coins plus loot at book value about 10.7k; 21-day live tallies): it should not bind an honest player at EA (unverified), and it bounds real-money-trade throughput; Security may lower the fuse |
| Same account | refused (`own_listing`), whatever the heroes | Storage moves items between your heroes for free, so a self-buy is only a fake price |
| Same network (accounts sharing a hashed IP within 24 h) | allowed, flagged in the ledger, left out of price history | households share an IP; Security sees the pattern |
| Never listable | worn gear and a running hunt's packed supplies (they are not in the bag: §4 #3, #4); the 6 untradeable items (Hearthfind trophies, Dragon Relic, Void Essence); the 16 worth-nothing items (the practice kit, the Oak Practice Blade, 3 recipe books, Air Rune, Bronze Arrows, Coarse Whetstone); locked items; store basics (Salted Shrimp is bought per use and is not an item); Crowns; anything bought with Crowns or given once per account (authoring rule: such items are untradeable and worth nothing) | no faucet can turn into market gold: premium's daily weapon, new heroes' kits and gifts are worth nothing; Crowns-for-gold stays the separate, capped exchange that needs Steam and legal review |

### 2026-10-21 Armour weights (K7, lane b-armour-weights): RULED
**Model.** Each worn piece's armour value (`defB`) feeds its own weight's line: heavy = Absorb (share of a landed blow), medium = Dodge (the blow misses outright), light = Ward (a flat amount off each landed blow). A mixed set is exactly the sum of its pieces, no penalty. Absorb and Dodge are `maxPm × P / (P + k × Level)` against the hero's OWN Level, so own-tier armour holds its worth and an old set fades. One order in `damage.js`: dodge, absorb, resistances (the element permille), ward, then the spell shield and HP; a total cap of 60% over the chain (dodge and absorb together, and a landed blow always keeps its share). Rows: `core-model.js ARMOUR_MITIGATION` (dodge 650/0.45, absorb 750/0.7, ward 1.5 per point, cap 600). Derived, never stored: `src/core/mitigation.js`, written on the hero's unit each swing boundary; the Character sheet shows the engine's Dodge / Absorb / Ward.
**`def` keeps one job:** the monster's to-hit roll, now Defense skill + the defence of gear OUTSIDE the weights (shield, cape) + buffs. Weighted armour left it: plate's defB put an armoured hero at the 5% to-hit floor from tier 3 up (an L40 Warrens party's Healer spent 0 g/h), so armour weight could never be a choice. Defense XP: a dodge pays the plain rate (not a shield block); a status tick (`dot`) pays none (Tyler 10-22).

### 2026-10-22 Coppercap Warrens at its floor (CONFLICTS item 2): RULED
